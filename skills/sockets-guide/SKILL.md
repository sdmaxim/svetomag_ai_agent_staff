# Lamp sockets (bases)

## Screw sockets (Edison screw, the number = diameter in mm)
- **E27**: the standard large screw (27 mm). Most table lamps, floor lamps, pendants, ceiling lights. 230 V.
- **E14**: the small screw (14 mm). Chandeliers, wall lights, small lamps; often candle or small-ball bulbs. 230 V.
- **E40**: the very large screw (40 mm). Street lights, warehouses, high-power lamps.
- E10: tiny screw, flashlights and decorations.

## Pin and twist sockets
- **GU10**: two thick pins with "feet"; push in and twist. 230 V spotlights.
- **GU5.3** (MR16): two thin straight pins 5.3 mm apart. Usually **12 V** with a transformer, but 220-230 V versions also exist -- the voltage must match the customer's system.
- **G9**: two looped pins on a small capsule. 230 V.
- **G4**: two small straight pins. **12 V** capsule.
- **R7s**: double-ended linear halogen tube for floodlights and uplighters; comes in lengths such as 78 mm and 118 mm, so the length must match.
- **GX53**: flat disc lamp with two pins, for slim recessed ceiling lights.

## Fluorescent tubes and compact lamps
- **G13**: T8 tubes (26 mm thick), pins 13 mm apart. Common lengths 60 cm (18 W) and 120 cm (36 W).
- **G5**: T5 tubes (16 mm thick), pins 5 mm apart.
- **2G11, G23, G24**: compact fluorescent lamps with plug-in bases. The exact code must match.

## When a customer does not know the socket
Ask ONE of these:
1. "Is it a screw base, or pins?" Screw: "large (E27) or small (E14)?" Pins: "push-and-twist (GU10) or two straight thin pins (GU5.3/G4)?"
2. "Can you read the text printed on the old bulb?" It usually shows the socket code and watts, e.g. "E14 40W".

## How to search the catalogue
- Use the code exactly as the `socket` filter: "E27", "E14", "GU10", "GU5.3", "G9", "G13", "R7s"...
- 12 V sockets (GU5.3, G4): mention that they need a transformer.

## Be careful
- Never say two different socket codes are compatible (E27 vs E14, GU10 vs GU5.3, G13 vs G5 are NOT interchangeable).
- Adapters and rewiring questions: pass them to a specialist.
